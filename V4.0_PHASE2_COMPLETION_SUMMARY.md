### 1. Genomic Meta-Analysis 🧬 ✅

**File:** `frontend/modules/genomic_ma.R` (600 lines)

**What It Does:**
- Meta-analysis of genome-wide association studies (GWAS)
- Gene expression meta-analysis
- Genetic variant synthesis across populations
- Opens Metanew to **precision medicine** and **genomics research** markets

**Key Capabilities:**
- ✅ GWAS summary statistics import (PLINK, BOLT-LMM, SAIGE, METAL formats)
- ✅ Quality control filters (MAF, imputation quality, HWE)
- ✅ Fixed/random effects meta-analysis for millions of SNPs
- ✅ Genomic control (λ correction for population stratification)
- ✅ Manhattan plot (interactive, genome-wide visualization)
- ✅ QQ plot (assess systematic bias and inflation)
- ✅ Regional association plots (zoom into genomic loci)
- ✅ Gene annotation (map SNPs to nearest genes)
- ✅ LD clumping (identify independent genetic signals)
- ✅ Gene-set enrichment analysis (GO, KEGG, Reactome pathways)
- ✅ Export to LocusZoom format
- ✅ Publication-quality plots (300 DPI Manhattan/QQ)

**Statistical Methods:**
- Inverse variance fixed-effects meta-analysis
- DerSimonian-Laird random-effects
- Genomic inflation factor (λ_GC) calculation
- Genome-wide significance threshold (p < 5×10⁻⁸)
- LD r² clumping for independent signals

**Clinical Impact:**
- **Precision Medicine:** Identify genetic variants associated with disease/drug response
- **Polygenic Risk Scores:** Meta-analytic evidence for PRS development
- **Drug Targets:** Identify genes for therapeutic targeting
- **Pharmacogenomics:** Personalized medicine based on genetics

**Example Use Case:**
```
Type 2 Diabetes GWAS Meta-Analysis:
- 5 cohorts (n=120,000 total)
- 8 million SNPs after QC
- 47 genome-wide significant loci (p < 5×10⁻⁸)
- Top SNP: rs7903146 (TCF7L2 gene)
  - OR = 1.38 (95% CI: 1.32-1.44)
  - I² = 12% (low heterogeneity)
- Gene-set enrichment:
  - Insulin secretion pathway (FDR = 0.001)
  - Beta-cell development (FDR = 0.02)

Clinical Relevance: TCF7L2 variants inform precision approaches
for diabetes prevention and treatment selection.
```

**Market Opening:**
- Academic genomics labs
- Precision medicine initiatives
- Pharmaceutical genetics research
- Population health genomics

**Technical Excellence:**
- Handles millions of SNPs efficiently (data.table)
- Multiple GWAS format support
- Interactive Manhattan plots (plotly)
- Comprehensive QC pipeline

### 2. Diagnostic Test Accuracy Network Meta-Analysis 🔬 ✅

**File:** `frontend/modules/dta_nma.R` (520 lines)

**What It Does:**
- Network meta-analysis for diagnostic tests
- Bivariate modeling of sensitivity and specificity
- Ranks multiple diagnostic tests simultaneously
- Extends beyond pairwise DTA to network synthesis

**Key Capabilities:**
- ✅ Bivariate random-effects model (sens + spec correlation)
- ✅ HSROC (Hierarchical Summary ROC) model
- ✅ Multiple test comparison (3+ tests in network)
- ✅ SROC plots for all tests (overlaid curves)
- ✅ ROC space visualization with confidence regions
- ✅ Test rankings by multiple metrics:
  - Diagnostic Odds Ratio (DOR)
  - Youden Index (sens + spec - 1)
  - Positive/Negative Likelihood Ratios
  - Clinical utility at target prevalence
- ✅ PPV/NPV calculation at specified prevalence
- ✅ Number Needed to Diagnose (NND)
- ✅ League table (all pairwise test comparisons)
- ✅ Network diagram visualization

**Statistical Methods:**
- Bivariate normal model for logit(sens) and logit(spec)
- Accounts for within-study correlation
- Between-study heterogeneity modeling
- Network consistency framework

**Clinical Impact:**
- **Test Selection:** Identify optimal diagnostic test for clinical use
- **Resource Allocation:** Prioritize tests by cost-effectiveness
- **Guideline Development:** Evidence-based test recommendations
- **Quality Improvement:** Replace inferior tests

**Example Use Case:**
```
COVID-19 Diagnostic Tests Network NMA:
Tests: RT-PCR, Rapid Antigen A, Rapid Antigen B, Antibody, CT

Network Results:
Test            | Sens (95% CI)    | Spec (95% CI)    | DOR    | Rank
----------------|------------------|------------------|--------|-----
RT-PCR          | 0.95 (0.91-0.97) | 0.99 (0.97-1.00) | 1831   | 1
Rapid Antigen A | 0.85 (0.79-0.90) | 0.97 (0.94-0.99) | 151    | 2
Rapid Antigen B | 0.78 (0.71-0.84) | 0.98 (0.95-0.99) | 178    | 3
Chest CT        | 0.88 (0.82-0.92) | 0.75 (0.68-0.81) | 21     | 5
Antibody Test   | 0.65 (0.58-0.72) | 0.96 (0.92-0.98) | 44     | 4

At 10% prevalence:
- RT-PCR: PPV = 91%, NPV = 99% → Best overall
- Rapid Antigen A: PPV = 76%, NPV = 98% → Acceptable for screening

Clinical Decision: RT-PCR remains gold standard, but Rapid
Antigen A acceptable for point-of-care screening.
```

**Market Opening:**
- Diagnostic test manufacturers
- Clinical laboratories
- Guideline development organizations
- Regulatory agencies (test approval)

**Technical Excellence:**
- Proper bivariate modeling (not naive univariate)
- Handles within-study sens/spec correlation
- Multiple ranking metrics (not just DOR)
- Clinical utility at realistic prevalence

### 3. Real-World Evidence Integration 🌍 ✅

**File:** `frontend/modules/rwe_integration.R` (650 lines)

**What It Does:**
- Combines RCTs with observational real-world evidence (RWE)
- Transportability methods to generalize RCT results
- Bias adjustment for observational studies
- Balances internal validity (RCTs) and external validity (RWE)

**Key Capabilities:**
- ✅ Study classification (RCT vs RWE)
- ✅ Target population definition (from RWE or external data)
- ✅ Effect modifier identification (age, comorbidities, severity)
- ✅ Transportability methods:
  - Inverse odds weighting
  - Doubly robust estimation
- ✅ RWE bias adjustment:
  - Propensity score weighting
  - Propensity score matching
  - Inverse probability weighting
  - Doubly robust estimation
- ✅ Covariate balance assessment (Love plot)
- ✅ Transportability weight diagnostics
- ✅ Integrated RCT+RWE meta-analysis
- ✅ Sensitivity to unmeasured confounding (E-value)
- ✅ Subgroup analysis by effect modifiers
- ✅ External validity assessment

**Statistical Methods:**
- Inverse odds transportability weighting
- Propensity score methods for confounding control
- Doubly robust estimation (outcome + propensity models)
- E-value calculation for sensitivity analysis
- Weighted meta-analysis

**Clinical Impact:**
- **Generalizability:** Transport trial results to real-world populations
- **Effectiveness:** Complement efficacy (RCTs) with effectiveness (RWE)
- **Regulatory:** FDA/EMA increasingly require RWE
- **Health Policy:** Real-world impact assessment

**Example Use Case:**
```
Drug X Effectiveness in Real-World Practice:

RCT Meta-Analysis (5 RCTs, n=5,000):
- RR = 0.70 (95% CI: 0.62-0.79)
- Population: Age 50-65, no comorbidities, closely monitored

Real-World Evidence (3 observational, n=50,000):
- Crude RR = 0.82 (95% CI: 0.77-0.88)
- Population: Age 40-75, 40% with comorbidities, routine care

Target Population: Real-world patients (age 40-75, mixed comorbidities)

Transportability Analysis:
- Effect modification by age: Minimal (p=0.42)
- Effect modification by comorbidities: Moderate (p=0.04)
- Covariate balance after weighting: SMD < 0.1 for all covariates

Bias-Adjusted RWE:
- Propensity score weighted RR = 0.76 (95% CI: 0.70-0.83)
- E-value = 2.1 (robust to moderate unmeasured confounding)

Integrated Estimate (Transported RCT + Adjusted RWE):
- RR = 0.74 (95% CI: 0.69-0.80)
- I² = 28% (acceptable heterogeneity)

Conclusion: Drug X is effective in real-world population (RR=0.74),
accounting for both internal validity (RCTs) and external validity (RWE).
26% risk reduction maintained in routine clinical practice.
```

**Market Opening:**
- Regulatory agencies (FDA RWE Program)
- Health technology assessment
- Pharmaceutical outcomes research
- Health policy and payer evidence

**Technical Excellence:**
- Transportability framework (Dahabreh et al.)
- Multiple bias adjustment methods
- E-value sensitivity analysis
- Covariate balance diagnostics
- Doubly robust estimation
