**File**: `data/nma_datasets/network_meta_analyses.csv`
**Size**: 50 comprehensive network meta-analyses
**Unique**: Multi-treatment comparisons with treatment rankings

**Coverage**:
- **Therapeutic Areas**: Psychiatry (20%), Rheumatology (16%), Cardiology (14%), Oncology (12%), Neurology (10%), Gastroenterology (8%), Pulmonology (6%), Other (14%)
- **Network Sizes**: Small (3-6 treatments, 20%), Medium (7-12 treatments, 50%), Large (13-21 treatments, 30%)
- **Largest Network**: 21 antidepressants for major depression (NMA001)

**Network Geometries**:

**Star Network** (40% of NMAs):
```
        Drug A ----\
        Drug B -----\
        Drug C ------ PLACEBO
        Drug D -----/
        Drug E ----/
```
- Most studies compare active vs placebo
- Few head-to-head trials
- Common in early drug development

**Mesh Network** (35% of NMAs):
```
    A --- B --- C
    |  X  |  X  |
    D --- E --- F
```
- Many direct comparisons between treatments
- Robust evidence base
- Lower reliance on indirect evidence

**Connected Network** (25% of NMAs):
```
    A --- B --- C
          |
          D --- E
```
- All treatments connected via paths
- Some gaps in direct evidence
- Mix of direct and indirect comparisons

**Data Structure**:
- `nma_id`: NMA001-NMA050
- `review_title`: Full NMA title
- `outcome`: Primary outcome
- `n_studies`: Number of RCTs in network
- `n_participants`: Total participants
- `n_treatments`: Number of treatments compared (3-21)
- `network_geometry`: Star, Mesh, or Connected
- `reference_treatment`: Common comparator (placebo/standard)
- `effect_type`: OR, RR, HR, MD, SMD, Rate Ratio
- `treatments_in_network`: Full list of all treatments
- `direct_comparisons`: Number of pairs with head-to-head trials
- `indirect_comparisons`: Number of pairs inferred indirectly
- `total_comparisons`: All possible pairwise comparisons
- `consistency_model`: NMA-consistency
- `inconsistency_p`: P-value testing direct = indirect (>0.05 good)
- `heterogeneity_within_designs`: Heterogeneity in direct comparisons
- `heterogeneity_between_designs`: Additional heterogeneity from mixing
- `tau_squared`: Between-study variance
- `overall_i_squared`: Overall heterogeneity %
- `best_treatment_rank1`: Treatment most likely #1
- `best_treatment_prob_rank1`: Probability treatment is best (0-1)
- `sucra_values`: Surface Under Cumulative Ranking for top treatments
  - SUCRA 100% = certainly best
  - SUCRA 50% = average
  - SUCRA 0% = certainly worst
- `league_table_available`: All pairwise comparisons matrix (Yes/No)
- `treatment_rankings_available`: Full ranking with probabilities (Yes/No)
- `ranking_plot_available`: Visual rankogram (Yes/No)
- `network_plot_available`: Network diagram (Yes/No)
- `certainty_assessment`: GRADE certainty (High/Moderate/Low/Very low)
- `publication_year`: Year published
- `funding_source`: Academic, Industry, Mixed, Government

### 4. Multilevel Meta-Analyses ✅

**File**: `data/multilevel_datasets/multilevel_meta_analyses.csv`
**Size**: 10 multilevel meta-analyses with 100+ correlated effect sizes
**Format**: Long format (one row per effect size)
**Unique**: Hierarchical variance components and within-study correlations

**Coverage**:
- **Therapeutic Areas**: Psychiatry (30%), Pain medicine (20%), Endocrinology (20%), Cardiology (10%), Oncology (10%), Dermatology (10%)
- **Multilevel Structures**:
  - Outcome + Time (40%): Multiple outcomes at multiple timepoints
  - Outcome + Time + Subgroup (30%): Full 3-level hierarchy
  - Outcome + Subgroup (20%): Multiple outcomes across populations
  - Time + Dose (10%): Dose-response over time

**What is Multilevel Meta-Analysis?**

Traditional meta-analysis assumes:
- One effect size per study
- Independent effect sizes across studies

Multilevel meta-analysis handles:
- **Multiple outcomes** per study (pain, function, QOL)
- **Multiple timepoints** (4, 8, 12, 24, 52 weeks)
- **Multiple subgroups** (age, sex, disease severity)
- **Nested comparisons** (dose levels, intervention variants)
- **Correlated effects** within studies

**Three-Level Variance Structure**:
```
Level 1: Sampling variance (σ²) - known, from SE
Level 2: Within-study variance (τ²_2) - between outcomes/timepoints
Level 3: Between-study variance (τ²_3) - heterogeneity across studies
```

Example from ML001 (Depression):
- **Level 1** (σ²): 0.42 - Measurement error
- **Level 2** (τ²_within): 0.28 - Variation between outcomes/timepoints
- **Level 3** (τ²_between): 0.15 - Variation between studies
- **Correlation** (ρ): 0.60 - Effects from same study correlated

**Interpretation**: 65% of heterogeneity is within studies (different outcomes/times), 35% is between studies.

**Data Structure** (one row per effect size):
- `multilevel_id`: ML001-ML010
- `review_title`: Full multilevel MA title
- `study_id`: Individual study identifier
- `study_author`: First author and year
- `year`: Publication year
- `n_intervention`, `n_control`: Sample sizes
- `outcome_domain`: Broad category (e.g., "Depressive symptoms")
- `outcome_name`: Specific measure (e.g., "HAM-D total score")
- `time_point_weeks`: Follow-up duration
- `subgroup`: Population subgroup (e.g., "PD-L1 ≥50%")
- `effect_size_type`: MD, Log RR, Log HR, SMD
- `effect_size`: Point estimate
- `variance`: Sampling variance (Level 1)
- `se`: Standard error
- `ci_lower`, `ci_upper`: 95% CIs
- `level_1_variance`: Sampling variance (known)
- `level_2_variance_within_study`: Within-study variance (estimated)
- `level_3_variance_between_study`: Between-study heterogeneity (estimated)
- `correlation_within_study`: Correlation between effects from same study (ρ)
- `total_outcomes_per_study`: Number of distinct outcomes
- `total_timepoints_per_study`: Number of follow-up assessments
- `total_subgroups_per_study`: Number of subgroups
- `multilevel_structure`: Description (e.g., "outcome+time+subgroup")
- `therapeutic_area`: Medical specialty
- `data_type`: IPD or Aggregate

**Typical Correlations**:
- **Same outcome, different times**: ρ = 0.60-0.80
- **Different outcomes, same domain**: ρ = 0.50-0.70
- **Different domains**: ρ = 0.30-0.55

**Use Cases**:
1. **Comprehensive Evidence Synthesis**: Extract ALL outcomes and timepoints (3-5× more data per study)
2. **Time-Course Modeling**: Model trajectory of treatment effects, identify peak timepoint, predict long-term from short-term
3. **Dose-Response Analysis**: Model dose-response, identify minimum effective dose, balance efficacy vs safety
4. **Subgroup Analysis**: Identify patient characteristics modifying treatment benefit (personalized medicine)
5. **Machine Learning**:
   - Predict effect sizes for new outcomes not yet studied

   - Forecast long-term outcomes from short-term data
   - Learn correlations between outcome domains
